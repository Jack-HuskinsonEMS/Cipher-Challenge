Dear Mr. Eliot,

I understand from our mutual friend, Professor Vandiver, that you are currently offering to undertake investigations that require discretion on both our parts. While I would usually prefer to engage the services of someone with more experience, there are reasons which I will not disclose now why he thinks you may be able to assist us. 

I represent a group of European investors who are financing an aviation documentary being produced by Monarch Pictures, and who are now facing the loss of their investment owing to the mysterious disappearance of important film footage assembled by the producer and director. The footage had taken considerable time and effort to assemble and was stored in six sealed cans. Some of it had been commissioned specifically for this motion picture, but when the can containing that material was opened in the cutting room, it was discovered that the film contained no images—it was as if the emulsion had never been exposed. This, of course, is preposterous, and I can only conclude that the original footage has been stolen, though how and by whom I cannot tell. 

What I can say is that the value of the missing footage is immensely increased by the fact that it is connected with plans for an overseas commercial venture dependent upon the success of this picture. While the film itself is unlikely to make money, it was hoped that it would play a significant role in promoting American achievements in aviation, and that the resulting overseas sales would provide a handsome return for the investors. 

While this might appear to require only the services of one of the many private detective agencies in Los Angeles, one peculiar feature of the case calls for additional skills that Professor Vandiver believes you can provide. Our chief suspect, a junior member of the photographic team, disappeared at the same time as the film footage. He had been lodging at a boarding house near the studio and appears to have left in such haste that he overlooked one clue: an apparently enciphered letter. 

As you have already determined, my letter to you is also enciphered, and I apologise for that. It must have been a surprise to open a document containing an unintelligible string of letters, but if you possess the skills that Professor Vandiver assures me you have, then this should not have presented too great a challenge. If, on the other hand, you do not, then this message will remain unread and I shall need to seek help elsewhere. 

Given the importance and value of this undertaking, and what Professor Vandiver has told me about you, I can assure you that the successful conclusion of this investigation will do much to secure your future as well as the interests of my clients.

Yours sincerely,  
Brenda Hartford
