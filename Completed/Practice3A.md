Most enjoyable. Mr Cassel was exceptionally kind and courteous and very appreciative of my paintings and sculptures. He asked if I would be willing to reproduce the inkwell I gave to you, reworked in silver. I promised that I would consider it, but am not at all certain that I will have the time.

Speaking of silver, I took advantage of Mr Cassel's expertise to ask him about the remarkable increase in the number of foreign coins circulating in France. I am afraid I did not entirely understand his answer, but apparently the price of silver has fallen so much that it is having the most extraordinary effects on money. I suppose that must also be why he asked me to make him a silver version of the chimera. When I first made it, I would never have dreamed of using silver since it would have cost a small fortune! Now it seems that silver is the new bronze.

But what I cannot understand is why, when silver is so cheap, there should be so few silver francs to be found. My purse contains more Greek and Swiss coins than French! Surely nobody would want to hoard francs when the silver itself is worth so little? I must ask Mr Cassel to explain it to me again when I next see him.

In any case, it seems there is a silver lining to the clouds gathering over the Bourse. Mr Eastman has been experimenting with the most remarkable new photographic films that can be rolled up instead of being kept on those dreadful glass plates. Francois is very excited about them, and I must say that, after all his troubles with broken plates, I can quite understand why. I really do think that if these new films are as successful as he hopes, we shall soon have no more need of glass plates at all. Would that not be wonderful?

I am looking forward to seeing you again soon. Mr Cassel has told me that he will be in Paris again when you next visit, so perhaps you will both come to the show? I hope so, for I think you will like my new hat!

Yours affectionately,
Sarah
